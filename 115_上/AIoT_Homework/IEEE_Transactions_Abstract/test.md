**Title—DietSense: A Data-Grounded and Safety-Aware Conversational AI Agent for Personalized Dietary Assessment and Weight Management**

**Abstract—** The increasing prevalence of overweight and obesity, affecting an estimated 2.6 billion adults worldwide in 2024, including 940 million individuals living with obesity, underscores the need for sustainable dietary monitoring. However, conventional dietary tracking systems require extensive manual input, including food identification, portion estimation, and ingredient decomposition. Self-reported dietary intake errors ranging from 18% to 54% further complicate nutritional assessment, particularly for mixed dishes and culturally specific meals. Although large language models (LLMs) enable natural-language interaction, their direct use for nutritional estimation introduces risks of hallucinated values, inconsistent calculations, and unsupported recommendations. To address these limitations, this paper proposes DietSense, a data-grounded, safety-aware conversational AI agent that integrates language-based interaction with domain-specific computational services. The proposed system adopts a tool-oriented architecture in which an LLM coordinates information retrieval and analytical operations through whitelisted function calls, while numerical calculations are delegated to deterministic modules. The framework integrates retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), USDA FoodData Central, the Taiwan Food Composition Database, and a dedicated vector database of Taiwanese cuisine. Its eight-module architecture encompasses speech input, intent classification, agent planning, data retrieval, nutritional analysis, domain knowledge integration, recommendation generation, and interactive reporting. A clarification mechanism resolves ambiguities in food identity, portion size, and preparation methods, while uncertainty quantification and rule-based safety constraints support reliable decision-making. Individual energy requirements are estimated using the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) calculations. The evaluation framework will employ 500 annotated Taiwanese food records, including night-market snacks, lunchboxes, noodle soups, mixed dishes, and beverages, supplemented by established nutritional databases. System assessment will examine estimation accuracy, uncertainty handling, interaction efficiency, and safety compliance. DietSense is designed to generate caloric and macronutrient ranges, individualized energy-balance assessments, dietary substitution recommendations, longitudinal trends, and traceable nutritional evidence rather than unsupported point estimates. The proposed architecture establishes a foundation for evaluating whether coordinated AI agents and domain-grounded analytical services can reduce dietary logging burden while improving the transparency, consistency, and safety of personalized nutritional decision support.

**Index Terms—** Conversational AI, domain-specific AI agents, dietary assessment, large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, uncertainty quantification, personalized weight management, safety-aware systems.

**Title—Context-Aware Restaurant Recommendation with Taichung Bus Accessibility: A Data-Grounded and Constraint-Aware AI Agent System**

**Abstract—** The increasing diversity of dining options in urban environments presents significant challenges for restaurant selection, particularly when users must consider both dining preferences and public transportation accessibility. According to a 2025 report published by the Taichung City Government, the number of registered food-service businesses in Taichung reached 22,612 by the end of 2024, representing a 47.55% increase compared with 2015. However, conventional restaurant recommendation approaches primarily emphasize user preferences, review relevance, geographical proximity, or historical interactions, without necessarily ensuring that recommended restaurants are practically accessible under specific transportation and temporal constraints. A restaurant may satisfy the intended dining context, such as a romantic date, birthday celebration, or group gathering, while requiring excessive walking, prolonged waiting, multiple transfers, or an arrival time incompatible with its operating hours. Although large language models (LLMs) enable natural-language requirement interpretation and semantic recommendation, their direct use for transportation feasibility assessment introduces risks of inconsistent temporal reasoning, unsupported assumptions, and unreliable constraint satisfaction. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a data-grounded, constraint-aware restaurant recommendation AI agent system that integrates dining-context understanding with public transportation accessibility analysis in Taichung, Taiwan. The proposed system adopts a context-first, transportation-aware recommendation architecture in which an LLM interprets user requirements, coordinates information retrieval, and generates evidence-grounded explanations, while transportation calculations, constraint verification, and recommendation ranking are delegated to deterministic computational modules. The framework integrates BM25, dense vector retrieval, hybrid semantic search, knowledge graph-based evidence representation, restaurant information, and the Transportation Data eXchange (TDX) platform for public transportation route planning and available bus arrival information. Its nine-module architecture encompasses data collection, entity extraction and resolution, knowledge storage, requirement understanding and agent planning, semantic restaurant retrieval, domain evidence integration, bus accessibility analysis, constraint verification and ranking, and interactive recommendation reporting. A clarification mechanism resolves ambiguities in dining preferences, departure locations, time specifications, budgets, and transportation restrictions, while a three-valued verification framework distinguishes satisfied, violated, and uncertain constraints. Temporal reasoning incorporates walking, waiting, riding, transferring, restaurant arrival, and dining duration to determine whether candidate restaurants remain feasible throughout the intended dining interval. The proposed evaluation framework will compare keyword-based, dense, and hybrid retrieval approaches, together with recommendation configurations that include or exclude transportation-aware filtering. System assessment will examine contextual retrieval relevance, transportation feasibility, constraint violation rates, verified recommendation coverage, and evidence traceability through representative dining scenarios and available restaurant and bus information. The system is designed to recommend up to three distinct restaurants accompanied by supporting contextual evidence, feasible bus transportation plans, estimated travel burdens, constraint verification records, and explicitly identified uncertainties rather than unsupported recommendations. The proposed architecture establishes a foundation for evaluating whether coordinated AI agents, domain-grounded information retrieval, and deterministic transportation verification can improve the practicality, transparency, and reliability of context-aware restaurant recommendations in real-world public transportation environments.

**Index Terms—** Context-aware recommendation, domain-specific AI agents, restaurant recommendation, large language models, public transportation accessibility, hybrid retrieval, knowledge graphs, temporal reasoning, constraint verification, explainable artificial intelligence.
